Actual Egg Timer

Privacy

Last updated 2 October 2026

Actual Egg Timer is a free app that I, Dan MacKinlay, make on my own as a personal project. It comes as a website and as an iPhone app. This page says what each one keeps about you, where, and how to delete it.

In short

Nothing you put into Actual Egg Timer leaves your phone or your browser. Neither app sends anything about you or your eggs to me or to anyone else.

What stays on your device

So that they work the same way next time, both apps keep:

  • your settings, such as the egg size, the units, your altitude, how much water you use, how you cool the eggs, and the language;
  • the cook in progress, so a running timer survives the page reloading or the app closing;
  • the eggs you rated: how each was cooked and how you said it came out, and what the app has learned from them;
  • how long your water took to boil, for each amount you have timed.

On iPhone, these are kept in the app’s own storage on the phone. If you back up your iPhone, the backup includes them, as it does any app’s data. On the web, they are kept in your browser’s storage for this site. Neither is ever sent anywhere.

Deleting it

  • iPhone: delete the app, and everything it kept goes with it.
  • Web: clear this site’s data in your browser’s settings.
  • Either app: Start learning again, in Settings, deletes the eggs you rated and the boil times, and keeps your settings.

What I don’t do

  • No accounts and no sign-in.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no cookies.
  • No code from other companies: no libraries, kits or trackers. Both apps are built only from the code in the project’s public repository, on Apple’s and your browser’s own software.
  • The iPhone app makes no network connections at all. The website loads only its own files, from this site.

Both apps have links, in Help and to the code, that open in your browser only when you tap them.

Alarms and the Lock Screen

On iPhone, the alarm is a notification that your phone schedules itself, and the timer on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island runs on your phone. Neither goes through any server, mine or Apple’s. On the web, the alarm is a sound the page plays.

Who else is involved

Netlify serves the website. Like any web host, it may keep ordinary request logs, such as your IP address, the time and the page you asked for, under its own privacy policy. I don’t run analytics on the site, and I don’t use those logs for anything.

Apple delivers the iPhone app, through TestFlight or the App Store, under its own privacy policy. If you have agreed, in your iPhone’s settings, to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass me crash reports and overall numbers. If you send feedback through TestFlight, it comes to me. Both come from Apple, not from the app.

Sharing results, later

I plan to add a way to share your egg results anonymously, so that everyone’s first guesses get better. It does not exist yet. When it comes, it will be off unless you turn it on. It will use a random number made on your device instead of your name, and you will be able to delete what you sent. I will update this page before it ships, and the date at the top will change.
